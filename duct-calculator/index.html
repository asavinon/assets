Wrap-around layout

Wrap a strip of paper (or measure along the circumference with a flexible tape) around the pipe. Square a reference line around the pipe at the throat. At each mark distance around the pipe, measure up from the reference line by the rise in the table, connect the points with a smooth curve, and cut. Mark 0 is the throat (short side); the middle mark is the heel (long side). If you set a throat length, use the "up from pipe end" column instead — every mark is measured straight up from the squared end of the pipe, so the throat lands exactly where you want it.

How this works: the duct's cross-section area (sq ft) comes from the diameter; velocity = CFM ÷ area. Velocity pressure VP = (V ÷ 4005)² in inches w.g. Straight-duct friction uses the standard galvanized duct equation 0.109136 × CFM1.9 ÷ D5.02 per 100 ft. A fitting's loss is its coefficient C × VP — smoother, longer-radius fittings have lower C. A rolling offset has two fittings, so double the fitting loss. CFM and velocity work both ways — enter either one and the other is calculated. Whichever you typed last is held when you change pipe size: hold a CFM and watch velocity move, or hold a design velocity (say 1500 FPM) and step through pipe sizes to see the CFM each size carries. What SMACNA is

SMACNA — the Sheet Metal and Air Conditioning Contractors' National Association — publishes the construction standards this trade builds to. The book that governs nearly everything in this app is HVAC Duct Construction Standards — Metal & Flexible (ANSI/SMACNA 006), which covers gauges, joints, seams, reinforcement, hangers, and sealing for rectangular, round, and oval duct. Related manuals: HVAC Air Duct Leakage Test Manual and HVAC Systems Duct Design. Standards, technical papers, and the store are at smacna.org. The tables below are field-reference summaries of the commonly used values — the manual and your project spec always govern.

Leakage class CL = leakage in CFM per 100 sq ft of duct surface at 1″ w.g., per the SMACNA HVAC Air Duct Leakage Test Manual. Lower is tighter — this is why sealed spiral round (CL 3) is the tightest system you can hang, roughly 8× tighter than minimally sealed rectangular.

Round duct field rules of thumb

Gauge schedule See the Airflow & Gauges tab — typical for ±2″ w.g. single-wall spiral
Negative pressure / higher class Usually one to two gauges heavier — check the manual's negative-pressure tables
Fittings Commonly two gauges heavier than the connecting pipe
Hangers & supports Typically max 12 ft spacing, straps/rods sized to diameter — SMACNA Ch. 5
Fitting joints Sealant on the male end, fasten per spec, pull sealant through the joint — don't just paint the outside
Elbow construction Die-stamped ≤ ~8″ · pleated/pressed mid sizes · gored (segmented) larger — gore count drives pressure loss
